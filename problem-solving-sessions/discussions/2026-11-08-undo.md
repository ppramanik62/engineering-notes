# 2026-11-08: undo and redo in a text editor

## The problem

A plain text notes app. People type, delete, paste, and replace selections. They want undo, as many steps back as they've taken, and redo to go forward again. No other requirements.

## How you thought about it

You started with the requirements rather than the data structure, which is the right end to start from. First thing you said was that undo looks linear, but then you walked a case out loud: undo twice, then type something new. From that one case you worked out it's a tree, not a line.

Then you asked your own question, and it was the good one: standing at a fork, redo has two children, so which one wins and how do you ever get back to the other? That's the real problem with keeping a tree, and you found it yourself before I pointed at it.

You tried long press for picking a branch, dropped it a minute later because it didn't fit a text editor, and moved to a panel beside the editor showing the tree. That's the same answer undotree and undo-tree arrived at.

Then we went down a level. First pass at a node was position plus the old text. When I asked whether that gets you forward again, you saw it didn't, though you briefly split one replace into two nodes along the way. Once we settled that select-and-type is one change, you closed it: position, old text, new text.

On cost you got to coalescing on your own. You said character by character would spam the tree, no editor does that, and proposed a debounce around 500ms. Then you broke your own grouping rule twice when I pushed: the cursor jumping ends a group, and switching between typing and deleting ends a group.

On find-and-replace you saw straight away that the node survives but position stops being a single number. And when I asked whether the index recorded for the 400th match is still right, you had it in one line: each replace shifts the later ones. Fix was yours too, go right to left.

## What worked

Walking a concrete case instead of reaching for a known structure. "Undo twice then type" is what produced the tree, and "the 400th match" is what produced the right-to-left fix. Every real move you made today came from running an example, not from recalling an answer.

You also dropped the long press without defending it. Being willing to throw away your own idea a minute after having it is worth more than the idea was.

## Where it got stuck

Two small places.

You went deep into interface design (long press, side panel) before the node had any contents. The question of what a node holds was open the whole time and the design can't be judged without it. I had to steer back down.

And when writing out the second node you gave "he", the resulting text, where the position goes. Small slip, but it's the delta model sliding into a snapshot model. The node stores a change, not a state. One question fixed it.

## Where we landed

A tree of edits, with the option to collapse it to a doubly linked list if you decide the discarded branch isn't worth keeping. Keeping it costs you a panel and a mental model the user has to learn, which is why most editors throw it away.

Each node holds a position (a list of them, in general), the old text, and the new text. That runs in both directions.

Nodes are grouped as the user works, and the group ends on a pause, a cursor jump, or a switch between inserting and deleting.

When a node carries many positions, apply it from the right so the earlier indices stay valid. Same going backwards.

## Worth coming back to

The delta-versus-snapshot line, since the node nearly slipped into holding a state. Worth asking of a design: is this storing what changed, or what things became, and what does each cost you when the thing is large?
